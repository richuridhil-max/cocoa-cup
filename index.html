Circular Engineering Science

The 5-Stage Scientific Manufacturing Cycle

How raw agricultural cocoa pod husks from Kerala farmers are transformed into high-performance, food-safe cups through a clean thermo-compression cycle.

1
Phase 01 • Raw Preparation

Industrial Solar & Thermal Drying

Freshly harvested cocoa pod husks collected from Kerala cocoa plantations arrive with ~80% moisture. They undergo clean hot-air dehydration in industrial continuous dryers, lowering moisture content down below 8% to preserve natural cellulose tensile strength and prevent microbial decay.

Phase 02 • Micronization

High-Energy Micron Milling

The dried cocoa husks are conveyed into specialized stainless steel impact pulverisers, grinding the fibrous husk into an ultra-fine, homogeneous organic powder (80–120 mesh). This micro-particulate size unlocks the natural lignin and gives the finished cup an extraordinarily smooth surface finish.

Phase 03 • Natural Formulation

Eco-Binder & Water Blending

The refined cocoa powder is blended with purified water and proprietary 100% plant-based edible binders in heated planetary mixers. This creates an elastic, cohesive organic matrix without the addition of any microplastic polymers, bisphenols, or chemical solvents.

Phase 04 • Thermo-Forming

Hydraulic Thermo-Compression

The blended compound is dosed into hardened tool-steel moulds mounted on high-tonnage hydraulic presses. Operating at 170°C and 180 bar pressure, the extreme heat and force induce natural thermo-polymerization of the lignin. This permanently bonds the fibers into a rock-solid, waterproof cup with a silky feel.

A Kerala Innovation for a Microplastic-Free World

The Dual Ecological Crisis

16 Billion Cups Discarded & Millions of Rotten Husks

Crisis 1: The Plastic-Lined Paper Lie. Conventional single-use paper cups are lined with a hidden 5% Polyethylene (PE) plastic coating to hold liquid. They cannot be recycled in regular paper facilities and take over 30 years to fragment into billions of toxic microplastics.

Crisis 2: Agricultural Waste Burden. Kerala cocoa farmers harvest millions of cocoa pods, but the beans account for only 25% of the pod's mass. The remaining 75%—the thick outer husk—is discarded in fields, generating methane and harboring fungal Phytophthora black pod blight.

The Circular Innovation

Upcycling Kerala Waste into Luxury Tableware

Our Breakthrough: We established a clean supply chain collecting fresh cocoa husks directly from farmers in Idukki, Wayanad, and Malabar. By applying precision micronization and high-pressure hydraulic thermo-moulding, we convert this agricultural biomass into 100% plastic-free cups.

The Result: High-performance disposable cups holding 100°C boiling drinks for hours, smelling subtly of chocolate, and degrading in backyards into nutrient-rich compost in just 90 days.
